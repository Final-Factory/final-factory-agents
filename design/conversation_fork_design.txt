Conversation forking: continuing one conversation somewhere else
===============================================================
Revision 1, 2026-09-07. Written after reading the ingest, scheduling, session and publish paths
in ffwatch.py, and the branch adoption design, which this deliberately copies the shape of.
Revision 2, 2026-09-08: written while implementing. Six corrections, and all of them are the
design meeting a shape the code already had.

  * `fork_conversation` takes the DESTINATION ROW, not a description of where to put one
    (section 9). Every ingress already makes that row before the directive is seen -- a thread's
    conversation is upserted before its messages, a channel message that continues nothing opens
    one, and the local ingress mints one -- so a signature carrying `into`, `kind`, `alias`,
    `venue` and a class would have been five arguments re-deriving what one row already answers.
    It also makes a refusal cheap: in a channel it leaves an ordinary conversation holding a
    gated message, exactly as a refused `!branch` does.
  * `ffwatch fork` is LOCAL ONLY; `--into` is gone (section 11). A fork into a Discord channel
    already has a door, and a second one here would have to re-derive that channel's watch alias
    to get the venue rule right, which is a second place for the one rule that must not be wrong
    to be wrong.
  * The fork is TITLED BY THE QUESTION under the directive, and by the source's title when there
    is none (5.6). `!conv 85` as a conversation name is a command in a list where every other
    row is a subject.
  * "In flight" is three signals, not one (5.5c). `conversation.state`, a queued or running turn
    row, and an unclaimed message. They disagree for a moment during a launch and the answer has
    to be the pessimistic one.
  * ffweb showing the link both ways moved from Tier B into Tier A. The page is being touched
    for the fork control anyway, and the control lands the operator on a page that would
    otherwise not say what it is a fork of.
  * Section 11's local fork mints its conversation id the way `submit` does, which walked into a
    pre-existing collision: milliseconds plus the pid is not unique inside one millisecond, and
    the second call is dropped by `INSERT OR IGNORE`. It was found and fixed independently
    upstream while this was being built (b7f55a8, `local_message_key`), and the local fork uses
    that rather than a second copy of it.

The four decisions in section 15 were taken as the design proposed them: nothing is posted in
the source thread, a branch gone from origin is a warning rather than a refusal, a public source
may be forked into another public channel, and the directive is `!conv` with `!conversation`
accepted for the same rule.


1. What this is for
-------------------

An operator is reading a public bug thread that has turned into real troubleshooting. The next
twenty messages are stack traces, branch names, half-formed theories and a container run that
edits code. None of that belongs in a channel players read. The thread cannot be moved either:
`conversation.thread_id` is UNIQUE and is the identity of the conversation, the session id is
derived from it (ffwatch.py:3290), and `reply_channel` (ffwatch.py:2398) sends every answer
back to the same place it came from.

Today the only way to carry on in private is to open a thread in #dev-chat and start again from
nothing. That conversation has no branch, no session and no memory of the investigation, so the
first turn re-reads what the old one already knew, and afterwards nothing in the database says
the two are about the same thing.

This design adds one thing: a conversation may be FORKED into another channel. The fork is a
new conversation that starts out holding what the original held. Its branch, its session
transcript and its history come across at the moment of the fork, and from then on the two run
independently. Nothing said in the original afterwards reaches the fork, and nothing said in the
fork ever reaches the original.

The name is chosen for the git meaning, and it is accurate. A fork is a copy taken at a point,
not a window onto the same thing.


2. What this is not
-------------------

It is not a redirect. The original conversation is untouched: it keeps its thread, its session,
its branch and its ability to answer. A player asking a follow-up in the public thread still
gets an answer there.

It is not a live mirror. The two sessions diverge at the fork and never re-converge. There is no
sync, no merge and no back-port, and section 12 says why adding one later is harder than it
looks.

It is not a way to make a conversation public. Section 7.1 gives the one-way rule.


3. What already works, and why the change is small
--------------------------------------------------

Most of this feature is already built, for other reasons.

  * `!branch <name>` is an operator directive with exactly the shape wanted here: a whole-line
    regex (ffwatch.py:2065), a handler that reads the author id off the stored row rather than
    off anything the text claims (`take_branch_directive`, ffwatch.py:4459), one writer shared
    with a CLI ingress (`adopt_branch`, ffwatch.py:8338), a gate on the message so a bare
    directive never becomes a turn, and one post back whether it worked or not.

  * The private venue exists and already means what is wanted. `watch.<alias>.venue: private`
    (config.md:178) reaches `turn.venue` per turn (ffwatch.py:5601), drops Max's persona from
    the prompt (`role_line`, ffwatch.py:8000), puts the pull request in the publish footer
    (ffwatch.py:10684) and turns a merge notice into plain developer prose (ffwatch.py:12775).

  * A branch is not exclusive. `conversations_on_branch` (ffwatch.py:8317) is written for
    several conversations on one branch, `adopt_branch` refuses only a branch with a turn
    actually in flight, and it names the other threads rather than refusing on their behalf.

  * Merge notices already fan out by branch. `conversations_for_pull_request`
    (ffwatch.py:12660) matches on `conversation.github_pr` OR on the branch, and `take_merge`
    queues a notice for every conversation it finds. A fork that inherits the branch therefore
    makes the public source thread hear about the merge without a line of new code, and it
    hears it in the player-facing register because `announce_merge` splits on the CONVERSATION's
    venue, not the fork's.

  * The blob store is content addressed and shared. `download_attachments` writes to
    `<state>/blobs/<xx>/<sha>` (ffwatch.py:4643) and `stage_attachment` copies from there into
    the conversation's attachment mount at job time, so a fork can serve the source's save files
    and logs with nothing copied and nothing re-downloaded from Discord's expired URLs.

  * The session already survives being rebuilt. When a transcript goes missing, build_job bumps
    the generation and seeds the new session from `render_summary` (ffwatch.py:7590-7605). That
    is the fallback path this design needs, already written and already exercised.

What is genuinely new: a directive that decides WHICH conversation a message opens, a copy of a
session transcript under a new id, a history query that reads across two conversations, and one
link column so the record says where a fork came from.


4. The shape
------------

`!conv 85` posted in a private channel by an operator creates conversation 91, which:

  * lives where the directive was posted, and is a conversation in that channel like any other;
  * carries `forked_from = 85`;
  * owns the same branch as 85, claimed through `adopt_branch`;
  * starts with a copy of 85's session transcript, so its first turn resumes rather than
    meeting the investigation cold;
  * reads 85's messages up to the fork point as its own history;
  * takes its venue and its container class from where it landed, decided by the rules that
    already decide those for any conversation opened there;
  * answers in its own channel, and only there.

Conversation 85 is not told, not gated, and not changed.


5. The directive
----------------

5.1 Grammar

    FORK_DIRECTIVE_RE = ^\s*!conv(?:ersation)?\s+(\d+)\s*$    MULTILINE

Anchored to a whole line and to the start of it, for the reason BRANCH_DIRECTIVE_RE is: a
sentence discussing the directive is prose and stays prose. First match wins. Both spellings the
same rule, because `!conv` is what gets typed and `!conversation` is what gets typed by somebody
who has forgotten whether the short form exists.

A Discord link instead of a number is a real convenience and is Tier B (section 14): resolve the
third segment of a message link against `conversation.thread_id` and `root_message_id`.

5.2 Where the hook goes, and why it is not where !branch's is

`take_branch_directive` runs inside `insert_message`, after the conversation has been chosen.
That is correct for a directive about a conversation and wrong for a directive that decides
WHICH conversation the message belongs to. The fork hook has to run earlier:

  * `ingest_channel_message` (ffwatch.py:5282), AFTER the "already ours" check and BEFORE
    `select_conversation`. Before the clustering window, or it attaches `!conv 85` to whatever
    the operator was already talking about in that channel and the fork is anchored on somebody
    else's conversation. After the dedupe, or the 15-minute sweep re-reads the directive and
    forks again every time it looks: `message.discord_id` UNIQUE is the only thing standing
    between this feature and one fork per sweep.

  * `ingest_thread` (ffwatch.py:4700), for the thread form. A thread whose first message is the
    directive becomes the fork itself: same conversation row `upsert_conversation` would have
    created, with the fork columns filled in and the inheritance done.

A directive in a channel NEVER joins a clustering window. It opens its own conversation,
anchored on the directive message, exactly as an unrelated new message would.

5.3 Operators only, and silent otherwise

Byte for byte the policy `take_branch_directive` states: the line is acted on only when
`is_operator(cfg, author_id)` says the Discord-authenticated author is one, and for anybody else
it is ordinary text. Not acted on, not refused, not mentioned. A refusal would confirm to a
stranger that the command exists, that this box has operators and that they are not one. The
host logs the attempt; the channel is told nothing.

5.4 The message that carries it

Same two rules as `!branch`, for the same reasons:

  * a message that is ONLY the directive is gated (`gate='fork_directive'`), so it never becomes
    a turn. `pending_messages` selects `gate IS NULL` (ffwatch.py:5786), which is the same door
    the engagement gate declines through.

  * a message that carries the directive AND a question keeps its turn only if the fork
    happened. A refused fork leaves the question standing in a conversation that does not have
    the branch or the session it was asked about, and answering it there is answering the wrong
    thing confidently.

A message carrying an accepted directive counts as ADDRESSED. Without that, a channel declared
`engage: mention` swallows `!conv 85` plus a question whenever the operator did not also ping the
bot: `is_addressed` (ffwatch.py:4378) keys on a mention or a reply to one of the bot's messages,
and `always_a_turn` reduces to that plus "evidence came with it". A directive is a command typed
at the harness, which is the thing `addressed` is trying to recognise, so the row is written with
`addressed=1` when the fork lands.

The directive line is left in the message text that reaches the model, as `!branch` is. Stripping
it would be tidier to read and would mean the prompt no longer matches the message row, which is
a worse trade in a system whose whole argument is that the record is what happened.

5.5 Refusals

Every one of these is a sentence posted back into the channel, never an exception:

  a. `there is no conversation <id>`
  b. `a conversation cannot be forked into itself`
  c. the source has a turn queued or running, or unclaimed messages waiting: refuse and name the
     source with a link. Two reasons at once. Its transcript is being appended to and a copy
     taken now can end mid-line, and its branch would be pushed by two runs racing for the same
     fast-forward. `adopt_branch` already refuses the second half; this refuses both, earlier,
     with one sentence.
  d. the source is at a private venue and this channel is public (section 7.1).
  e. this conversation already has a turn. The directive opens a conversation; it does not
     convert a live one. In a thread that means: open a new thread. In a channel it cannot
     happen, because the directive always anchors its own conversation.
  f. the source's branch is claimed by a conversation with a turn in flight. This is
     `adopt_branch`'s own refusal, passed through in its own words.

A source whose branch is no longer on origin is NOT a refusal. See 6.1.

5.6 The acknowledgement

One post either way, at the fork, addressed to nobody. At a private venue this is plain
developer prose and none of max-voice applies. Three facts, because three is what the operator
needs and there are exactly three:

    ok - this continues conversation [85](link). It is on branch `ffbox/join-desync-d85t2`,
    already under pull request [#412](url), and it has the session from that thread, so it
    starts where the last turn left off. This is an ffdev container.

and on the summary fallback (6.2), the third clause becomes `the session transcript could not be
copied, so it starts from a written summary of that thread instead`.

Nothing is posted in the source thread. Section 15 has the argument.


6. What the fork inherits
-------------------------

6.1 The branch

Through `adopt_branch(fork_id, source_branch, by=author_id)` and never by writing the column
directly. `adopt_branch` is the one writer for both existing ingresses and this is a third; the
five checks it performs are all still the right ones, and its `record_branch_pull_request` call
is what gives the fork the source's pull request without a second lookup.

A source with no branch gives the fork no branch, and the fork behaves like any other new
conversation: its first run that pushes claims one. The two are then linked by `forked_from`
alone, which is enough for a person and not enough for the merge-notice fan-out. See 12.2.

A branch that has been deleted from origin makes `adopt_branch` refuse with "is not on origin".
The fork still happens and the ack says the branch could not be taken and why. The alternative,
refusing the whole fork, would mean a finished piece of work cannot be forked to talk about, and
talking about it is a legitimate use. This is the one place the design chooses to adapt rather
than refuse, and section 15 flags it.

6.2 The session

The point of the whole feature. A conversation's value at turn nine is not its Discord messages,
which the database can rebuild at any time. It is the model's own trace: what it read, what it
ruled out, which approach it tried and abandoned. `render_summary` cannot rebuild any of that,
which is the argument the compaction design already makes at ffwatch.py:7552-7570.

The graft:

  1. Source file: `transcript_path(85, conv85.session_id)`, which is
     `<state>/conversations/85/claude/projects/<slug>/<sid>.jsonl`. `CONTAINER_PROJECT_SLUG` is a
     constant, so the two conversations use the same project directory name and only the file
     name differs.
  2. Fork session id: `session_id_for(fork_thread_id)`, generation 1. Deterministic, its own, and
     it cannot collide with the source's because thread ids differ.
  3. Copy the file to `transcript_path(fork_id, fork_sid)`, rewriting `sessionId` on every
     record. Measured against a real transcript on the build server (conversation 10, 135
     records): `sessionId` is present on all 135, including Claude Code's own bookkeeping lines
     that carry no uuid.
  4. Leave `uuid`, `parentUuid` and `leafUuid` alone. They are internal to the file, and the two
     files being identical there costs nothing: `_index_transcript` de-dupes by uuid WITHIN a
     conversation (ffwatch.py:9257), and these are two conversations.
  5. `cwd` and `gitBranch` inside the records stay true, because the workspace path is a constant
     and the branch is inherited.
  6. Record `fork_session` = the source session id, so the row says where the transcript came
     from and `fork_session IS NULL` means it was seeded from a summary instead.

One code change follows: `resume` is computed as `int(turn["seq"]) > 1 and os.path.exists(...)`
(ffwatch.py:7546), and a fork's first turn is seq 1. It becomes

    resume = os.path.exists(transcript) and (int(turn["seq"]) > 1 or conv["fork_session"])

and the branch below it that seeds a new generation from `render_summary` has to cover the same
case, so a fork whose graft failed or was lost still starts from something.

THIS NEEDS LIVE VERIFICATION before it is relied on, in the style of the /compact verification
recorded at ffwatch.py:7563: confirm that `claude -p --resume <new id>` accepts a transcript that
was copied and had its `sessionId` rewritten, and that the reply shows knowledge from the source
session. If Claude Code refuses it, the fallback is already written: seed generation 1 from
`render_summary` and say so in the ack. The feature is worth having either way; it is worth much
more if the graft works.

6.3 The history

Message rows are NOT copied. `message.discord_id` is UNIQUE, so a copy would have to mint fake
ids, and `ingest_channel_message`'s "already ours" check (ffwatch.py:5321) keys on exactly that
column. Instead the fork READS the source's messages:

  * `build_job`'s history query (ffwatch.py:7533) selects `conversation_id=?`; for a fork it
    selects across `(source_id, fork_id)`, bounded by `fork_source_watermark`, still capped by
    `history_messages`.
  * `render_summary` (ffwatch.py:7797) walks the same pair, so the summary fallback in 6.2
    renders the source's turns and not an empty document.

`fork_source_watermark` is the source's newest message id at the moment of the fork. It is what
makes "further messages in the original do not reach the fork" a fact about a query rather than a
promise. It also means the two can be read side by side later without one of them growing into
the other's record.

6.4 Attachments

Nothing to do. Blobs are content addressed under a single shared directory and `stage_attachment`
copies out of it at job build time. A fork asking for a save file the source was given gets the
same blob.

6.5 base_sha and the pull request

`base_sha` is copied, so the fork's first run starts from the same tree the source was working
from when its branch is absent. `github_pr` and `github_base` are not copied by hand:
`adopt_branch` finds the pull request on the branch and records it, which is both the same code
path an adopting thread already uses and the reason the ack can name the PR.

6.6 What is not inherited

Turns, runs, verifications, transcript_event rows, the outbound history, `read_through`,
`compacted_at_seq`, `github_issue`, the ack reaction state, and `in_watermark_id`. The fork is a
new conversation with a seq that starts at 1. Its web page shows its own runs, and the source's
page shows the source's.


7. What the fork chooses
------------------------

7.1 Venue, and the one-way rule

The fork's venue comes from where it lands, through the watch entry, like every other
conversation. `turn_venue` needs no change; it reads `conv["watch_alias"]`, and the fork has its
own.

The rule that matters is the direction:

    A fork may never be at a more open venue than its source.

Private into public is refused. Public into private is the case this feature exists for. Public
into public is allowed and is occasionally what you want, for instance moving a bug thread's
work into #agent-testing. Local (`shell`, `web`) and `github_pr` sources count as private,
because `turn_venue` already says they are.

The rule is about the SOURCE's venue and not about who is asking, and it is checked at fork time
only. Nothing re-checks it afterwards, because a fork is a copy: after the moment it is taken,
the source cannot leak into it and it cannot leak into the source. What the rule prevents is the
single moment where a private session's contents would be copied into a room that reads it.

7.2 The container class

Nothing here is a fork decision. The destination picks the class the way it already picks it for
any conversation opened there, and there is no third class and no fork-specific rule:

  * from Discord, `discord_agent_class(cfg, author_id)` (ffwatch.py:1580) on the operator who
    posted the directive, which resolves through `discord.operator_pool` to `ffdev`;
  * from ffweb, the agent selector the new-prompt form already has (ffweb.py:1854), which is the
    same one `ffwatch submit --agent` obeys;
  * from `ffwatch fork`, `--agent`, defaulting the way `submit` does.

`fork_conversation` takes the class as an argument and writes it. It does not choose it.

One consequence is worth stating rather than leaving to be discovered. A fork carries a
conversation's history into whatever class it lands in, so a fenced thread's messages can end up
in an unfenced container. That is not a hole this feature opens in `demote_for_stranger`
(ffwatch.py:4544), which guards against a stranger's text reaching an unfenced container by
ACCIDENT: a fork is a named conversation, named by somebody the box trusts, recorded with who and
when. It is the same judgement an operator makes when they paste a player's log into `ffwatch
submit`, which has always run in ffdev. Two things keep it honest either way: `demote_for_stranger`
applies to the fork from its first message like any other conversation, and the inherited history
stays behind the prompt fence whatever the container is (7.3).

7.3 Kind, and why a fork is never `direct`

The fork's `kind` comes from where it lands, not from the source. Inheriting it would route a
fork of a `github_pr` conversation's replies to a pull request (`record_reply`,
ffwatch.py:9402), and would give a fork of an `operator_dm` a kind with no channel.

`kind` also decides the SHAPE OF THE PROMPT through `DIRECT_KINDS` (ffwatch.py:2176): a direct
conversation's text is handed to the model with no `<discord>` fence and no untrusted-input
framing, because it was typed by somebody this box trusts. A fork's history was not. So:

    A fork of a conversation that was not direct is never direct, wherever it lands.

Carried by one column, `fenced_history`, set at fork time from `is_direct_conversation(source)`,
and read by `is_direct_conversation` before it consults the kind. There is exactly one caller
that passes a row (ffwatch.py:7635) and it is the one that matters.

That guard is what makes the two awkward destinations safe rather than refused: an operator DM,
and a local fork with no Discord side at all (section 11).


8. The columns (schema v18)
---------------------------

All additive, through `ADDED_COLUMNS` (ffwatch.py:328), which is the only migration shape this
daemon supports. Every reader guards for their absence the way `conversation_branch` does.

    conversation.forked_from             INTEGER   the conversation this one continues
    conversation.forked_at               TEXT
    conversation.forked_by               TEXT      Discord snowflake, or the unix login for the
                                                   local ingresses, same shape as
                                                   branch_adopted_by
    conversation.fork_source_watermark   TEXT      the source's newest message id at the fork;
                                                   bounds the inherited history
    conversation.fork_session            TEXT      the source session id the transcript came
                                                   from; NULL means it was seeded from a summary
    conversation.fenced_history          INTEGER   1 when the source was not direct (7.3)

No index. The lookups are by primary key in one direction and rare in the other.

No configuration key is added, so `ffbox/config.md` is unchanged.


9. The one writer
-----------------

`fork_conversation(fork_id, source_id, by)` returns `(ok, reason)` and is the only thing that
writes any of the columns in section 8. All three ingresses call it: the directive, `ffwatch
fork` and the page's fork control (section 11). This is the property `adopt_branch` has, and the
reason its rules have not come to differ by which door somebody used.

THE DESTINATION ROW ALREADY EXISTS when it is called, and that is what keeps the signature to
three arguments. Every ingress makes the row on its own path: ingest_thread upserts a thread's
conversation before its messages, ingest_channel_message opens one for a message that continues
nothing, and `open_local_fork` mints one. Class, kind, alias, venue and channel are read off it.

Order matters, because two of the steps can fail:

  1. read both rows, run the refusals in 5.5 cheapest first
  2. write the fork columns, guarded on `forked_from IS NULL`
  3. adopt the branch, and keep its sentence for the ack
  4. graft the transcript, falling back to a summary seed
  5. return the whole sentence, and let the caller post or print it

A failure at 3 or 4 leaves a usable fork and a sentence saying what it did not get. A refusal at
1 writes nothing: in Discord the destination stays an ordinary conversation holding a gated
message, and `open_local_fork` deletes the row it had just made, which it can do because nothing
outside ffwatch has been told that id.


10. What the link buys, today and later
---------------------------------------

Today, with nothing else built:

  * The merge notice reaches the public source thread when the fork's pull request merges. This
    already works through the shared branch (section 3), and it is exactly right: the reporter
    who asked in public hears that the fix shipped, in Max's register, with no pull request link,
    while the fork hears it as a developer.
  * `adopt_branch`'s "conversation 85 has worked on this branch before" line names the source
    when any later thread adopts the same branch.

With `forked_from` recorded, small things become possible and each is a few lines. The first is
built (revision 2); the rest are not:

  * ffweb shows the link both ways: `forked from …` on the fork's page, `forked into …` on the
    source's, each one a link, next to the branch the two of them share.
  * `ffwatch status` says it on the conversation line.
  * The merge fan-out could walk the fork chain as well as the branch, which matters only for a
    fork taken before the source had a branch (6.1).
  * A future `!conv` could refuse to fork a conversation that already has a live fork, or offer
    the existing one instead of making a second.

Deliberately not built: any flow of information from the fork back to the source. Section 12.


11. The other two ingresses
---------------------------

The directive is one door. `fork_conversation` (section 9) is the only writer, so the other two
are thin.

    ffwatch fork --conversation 85 [--agent CLASS]

The twin of `ffwatch adopt`, and the same argument for having it: a design where only Discord can
do a thing cannot be tested or recovered from a terminal.

LOCAL, AND ONLY LOCAL. Revision 1 also had `--into <thread>` for binding a Discord channel from
the terminal, and it is gone: a fork into a Discord channel already has a door, and a second one
here would have to re-derive that channel's watch alias to get the venue rule right. That is the
one rule that must not be wrong, and it should have one implementation. What comes back is a
conversation with no Discord side, continued with `ffwatch submit --conversation <fork>` or from
the web page.

ffweb gets a fork control on the conversation page, next to the follow-up box, and it shells out
to `ffwatch fork` the way every other action on that page shells out to `ffwatch`
(ffweb.py:1423-1445 is the pattern). Its default is `--local`: a fork made from the page is
answered on the page, which is the whole point of forking from there. It carries the same agent
selector the new-prompt form has.

A local fork is safe for the reason 7.3 gives. A local conversation is normally `direct`, and
`fenced_history` overrides that, so a public thread's untrusted text does not slip into the
trusted prompt shape by being forked to a terminal or a web page.

12. What is deliberately absent
-------------------------------

12.1 Any path back. Nothing the fork says or does reaches the source, except the merge notice,
which is a fact about origin rather than about the fork. A "post this answer back to the public
thread" flow is a real thing to want and belongs in a design of its own, because it needs a
composer that decides what is public-safe, and the harness already has one of those in
`record_private_half` (ffwatch.py:9139) that is worth reusing rather than reinventing.

12.2 Any re-sync. Two sessions that have both run cannot be merged; there is no operation on a
Claude Code transcript that means "take these two branches of reasoning and produce one". A
second `!conv 85` in a new thread takes a second fork from wherever 85 is now, which is the honest
version of a re-sync and costs nothing to allow.

12.3 Any exclusivity. A conversation may be forked twice, and a fork may be forked. The rules
compose: each fork takes the branch through `adopt_branch`, so only one of them may be running a
turn on that branch at a time, and that is enforced where it already is.


13. Failure modes
-----------------

  transcript missing at fork time       fork proceeds, seeded from render_summary, ack says so
  transcript half written               refused before it starts: 5.5c refuses a source with a
                                        turn in flight, which is when the file is being appended
  branch gone from origin               fork proceeds without it, ack says so (6.1)
  branch busy                           refused, adopt_branch's own sentence
  source is a local conversation        allowed; venue counts as private, fenced_history is 0
                                        because a local conversation IS direct, and the fork
                                        into a Discord channel is a demotion, not a promotion
  fork's channel is unwatched           cannot happen; ingest_event refuses an unwatched channel
                                        before either hook is reached
  two operators fork the same source
  at the same moment                    two forks, both valid, and the second one's adopt_branch
                                        refuses the branch if the first has already queued a turn
  ffwatch restarted mid-fork            the conversation row exists or it does not; steps 3 and 4
                                        are idempotent on re-run and there is no half state that
                                        a later message can act on


14. Tiers
---------

Tier A, the feature: the columns, `fork_conversation`, the two ingest hooks, the directive and
its refusals, the branch adoption call, the transcript graft with its fallback, the resume fix,
the history and summary queries, `fenced_history` in `is_direct_conversation`, the ack,
`ffwatch fork`, and ffweb's fork control.

Tier B, worth doing soon and not needed for the first use: the Discord-link form of the argument
(5.1), ffweb showing the link both ways (10), the merge fan-out walking the fork chain (10), and
`!conv` naming an existing fork instead of making a second.


15. Decisions for Ben
---------------------

  a. Does the source thread get told anything? The design says no, on the grounds that a line
     saying "continuing this elsewhere" tells a public channel that a private one exists, and
     that nothing is lost by silence because the source keeps working. The counter-argument is
     that a reporter watching a thread that goes quiet has no idea anyone is still on it. A
     third option: say nothing at the fork, and let the existing merge notice be the thing the
     public thread eventually hears.

  b. A branch deleted from origin: fork anyway with a warning, or refuse? The design chooses to
     adapt, which is the only place it does.

  c. Should `!conv` in a PUBLIC channel be allowed at all when the source is public? It is
     legitimate (moving work from a bug thread into #agent-testing) and it is also the shape most
     likely to be typed by accident. An alternative is to require the destination be private in
     v1 and lift it later.

  d. Is `!conv` the right name next to `!branch`? `!fork 85` says what it does; `!conv 85` says
     what it names.
