ffbox warm branches: a second tier of spare that gives its place back
=====================================================================

2026-09-06. Extends design/ffbox_idle_agents_design.txt, which built the pool this stands on.

The pool warms one branch per class. `pool_branch()` answers `pool_ref or base_ref`, the keeper
stages `idle` copies of that, and `pool_claim_for` hands one to a turn whose ref matches it
exactly. Every other turn launches cold and pays the forty seconds the pool exists to remove.

Which turns are those. `run_ref()` puts a conversation's OWN branch at the top of the ladder, so
from the moment a conversation pushes, every later turn of it asks for a branch the pool has never
heard of. That is not an edge: it is turn 2 onwards of every piece of dev work the box does. A
follow-up on a thread -- "actually, also do X" -- is exactly the question-shaped turn the idle
agents design was written for, and it is the one shape that always misses.

So: warm a handful of RECENTLY USED branches as well, out of the slack the box is not using, and
give those places straight back the moment anything else wants them.

    warm-held         what the pool stages today. `idle` per class, on the class's branch.
                      Nothing evicts it. That rule stands.
    warm-evictable    the new tier. Recently-touched branches, staged only into slack, shed
                      on demand, and worth nothing to anybody when it goes.

The whole design is the second word of the second name.


0. Sizing it
-------------

This buys forty seconds on a repeat turn against a standing cost in RAM that is paid always, so
how large `warm_branches.count` should be is a measurement rather than a taste. ffwatch.db on the
build server answers it:

    SELECT COUNT(*) FROM (
      SELECT LAG(COALESCE(t.started_at, t.queued_at))
               OVER (PARTITION BY c.branch ORDER BY t.queued_at) AS prev,
             COALESCE(t.started_at, t.queued_at) AS at
        FROM turn t JOIN conversation c ON c.id = t.conversation_id
       WHERE c.branch IS NOT NULL)
     WHERE prev IS NOT NULL
       AND julianday(at) - julianday(prev) < 1.0/24;

How many turns arrived on a branch that had one within the hour -- the turns this would have
served warm. Take it before raising the number.

IT IS NOT A PRECONDITION, and an earlier draft of this document made it one, seeding `count: 0`
until somebody had run the query. That is a feature nobody sees. The mechanism is the same at any
size, the reserve makes an over-large pool harmless rather than dangerous, and the honest default
is the one that does the thing that was asked for. It ships at ONE PER CLASS -- two on a box with
two classes, which reads "a warm sandbox or two" as the box's number rather than a class's.


1. What gets built
------------------

  1. A second tier of staged container, `warm-evictable`, staged onto branches that a turn used
     recently rather than onto the class's `base_ref`.
  2. A box-wide RESERVE: evictable spares may never occupy the last `workload_reserve` places
     under `max_concurrent_runs`. Anything that wants a place therefore finds one.
  3. A SHED: when free places fall below the reserve, the keeper destroys one evictable spare per
     pass, least-recently-used branch first, until the reserve is whole again.
  4. `warm-held` is unchanged in every respect, including that nothing evicts it.
  5. `warm_branches.count` is per class and ships at 1. `0` is exactly current behaviour, and
     is what a box that does not want this sets.

Not a goal: warming a branch nobody has asked about yet. The candidate set is drawn from what the
box has actually run, never from `git branch -r`. A prediction that is wrong costs 24 GiB.

Not a goal: making the FIRST turn of a conversation faster. That one already hits the held pool,
because a conversation with no branch asks for `base_ref` or for a pinned sha, and a pinned sha
matches any warm container of its class (`looks_like_sha` in `pool_claim_for`).


2. Why the eviction rule survives
----------------------------------

The idle agents design settled this on 2026-09-01 and the README states it flatly: NOTHING IS
EVICTED. A cold launch short of a place waits for one; it does not take a staged container to make
room for itself. The reasoning was that with two agent classes, eviction is one worker type taking
another's warm container, and the turn it is taken from then pays the forty seconds it was staged
to save. That reasoning is correct and this design does not touch it.

What it does is add a tier the reasoning does not cover. A held spare is a PROMISE: the config
said `idle: 1`, the keeper made one, and a turn of that class is entitled to expect it. An
evictable spare is a GUESS -- nobody asked for it, no configured number is short while it is
missing, and the turn it might have served does not exist yet and may never. Destroying a promise
to serve somebody else is the trade that was rejected. Destroying a guess is not that trade.

The tiers are also not interchangeable in the shed order, and that is the whole safety property:
the shed walks evictable spares and stops. It never reaches a held one, whatever the pressure. A
box under sustained load sheds its guesses, keeps its promises, and ends up exactly where it is
today.


3. The tier is a label, and a demotion is not a relabel
--------------------------------------------------------

`ffbox --stage-pool` gains `--pool-tier held|evictable`, which goes on as
`--label ffbox.pool.tier=...` beside the three labels already set there (ffbox:1351-1354).
`pool_containers()` reads it into each container's dict the way it already reads `ffbox.pool` and
`ffbox.agent.class`. An EMPTY label is `held`, which is the right answer for every container
staged before this existed: they were staged by the one tier there was.

DOCKER CANNOT RELABEL A RUNNING CONTAINER, so the label says what a container was staged AS and
can never say what it has become. One thing does need it to change: when a class's `base_ref` or
`pool_ref` moves, the held spares on the old branch instantly stop being the promise anybody
made -- the class's pool is now short by one on the NEW branch, and the old container serves
nothing. Leaving those at the front of the "never shed this" queue would have the box protecting
containers it has no use for while shedding ones it does.

So the tier a shed decision uses is COMPUTED, not read:

    effective_pool_tier(c) = "evictable" if c["branch"] != pool_branch(c["class"])
                             else (c.get("tier") or "held")

A held spare whose branch no longer matches its class's pool branch is demoted, once, by
arithmetic. Nothing is written anywhere. The label stays honest about creation and the demotion
stays honest about now.

`.get("tier")` AND NOT `c["tier"]`, which is not fussiness. An absent tier already has a correct
answer -- held, the one tier there was -- so requiring the key would turn a container dict built
without it into a crash in the keeper, for no gain.


4. Which branches
------------------

The candidate list comes out of the database on each keeper pass, ordered most recent first:

    SELECT c.branch, c.agent_class,
           MAX(COALESCE(t.ended_at, t.started_at, t.queued_at)) AS seen
      FROM turn t JOIN conversation c ON c.id = t.conversation_id
     WHERE c.branch IS NOT NULL
       AND c.state <> 'closed'
     GROUP BY c.branch, c.agent_class
    HAVING seen > :cutoff
     ORDER BY seen DESC

`conversation.branch` is the right column and `run.branch` is not: the conversation's is written
only after a push succeeds, so it names a branch that exists on origin, while a run's is the name
it was launched with whether or not anything came of it.

`COALESCE(ended_at, started_at, queued_at)` IS THE LATEST THING THAT HAPPENED TO THE TURN. The
three timestamps only ever increase, so the coalesce picks the most recent of them: when it
finished, else when it began, else when it was queued. A queued turn counts as an access because
it is one -- that turn is about to want this branch, which is the strongest signal in the table.

CORRECTED 2026-09-06, hours after this shipped. It read `COALESCE(started_at, queued_at)` and was
wrong for exactly the case the tier exists to serve. ffdev's `agent_secs` is two hours with warm-up
and verification either side, so a long dev turn that started three hours ago and ended a minute
ago was dated three hours ago: its branch fell outside the one-hour window at the very moment the
run finished, which is when a follow-up is most likely and the whole reason that branch was worth
warming. The tier served short turns and silently skipped every long one -- the opposite of the
selection it was written for, and invisible, because a branch that is not a candidate produces no
log line.

Three filters on top, all of them mandatory:

  a. NOT the class's own `pool_branch()`. Those are the held tier's job and are counted there.
  b. `mirror_carries(branch)` (ffwatch.py:7483). The container reaches no network and fills from
     the local mirror, and a branch gets into the mirror by luck of a CI fetch having run. Staging
     on a branch the mirror lacks produces a container that dies inside restore-workspace.sh, which
     the keeper then replaces, which dies -- a staging loop that only `pool_stage_backoff_secs`
     stops, burning a 22 GiB extraction each time round. This check is not an optimisation.
  c. Not already staged. One evictable spare per (branch, class); a second is a second guess about
     the same thing.

`mirror_take()` exists and could push a missing branch into the mirror. It is deliberately NOT
called here. That is the launch path's business, done for a turn that is waiting; doing it
speculatively means the box mirrors branches on the strength of a guess.

Per class, because everything else about a spare is: the network, the plugins, the clocks and the
credential all come from the class block, and `pool_claim_for` matches on class first. A branch
that both classes touched gets a spare in each or in neither, decided by each class's own numbers.


5. The reserve, and why there is no demand signal
--------------------------------------------------

Nothing on this box can ask for a place. `slot.sh` polls `ffbox_workload_has_room` every
`pool_poll_seconds` (5) and logs "the box is at its container ceiling; waiting" for as long as it
takes; a cold `ffbox` run exits 77; `schedule()` breaks out of the queued loop and tries again next
pass. There is no channel from any of them to the keeper, and adding one would mean a lock, a file
and a race between the party that writes it and the party that clears it.

The reserve makes the signal unnecessary. State it as one invariant the keeper maintains:

    an evictable spare may exist only while free places would remain >= workload_reserve

Then the sequence takes care of itself:

    free = 2, reserve = 1     the keeper may hold one evictable spare
    CI's slot.sh wants a place -> it finds one, because the reserve was never spent
    free = 0                  the invariant is broken
    next keeper pass          sheds one evictable spare
    free = 1                  the invariant holds again, and the next waiter finds a place

The pool shrinks by exactly one per demand event, driven by demand, with nobody signalling
anything. The waiter's latency is one keeper pass plus one `docker stop` -- the stop is floored at
`LICENCE_STOP_FLOOR` (120s) so PID 1's traps run, but nothing waits on it: `pool_expire()` already
hands its stops to a thread and the shed uses the same pattern.

`workload_reserve` is box-wide and not per class, because the thing it protects is box-wide. CI
does not know what an agent class is.

WHY NOT APPLY THE RESERVE TO HELD SPARES TOO. It is tempting: today the keeper stages a held spare
whenever `workload_room() > 0`, so two held spares can take the last two places on the box and sit
there for four hours while CI polls. That is a real latent problem and this design does not fix it,
because fixing it means `idle: 1` sometimes producing zero spares -- a configured promise quietly
not kept, which is worse than the queueing it would prevent, and a change to the held tier's
contract that has nothing to do with warm branches. Worth its own decision; noted in section 12.


6. Shedding
------------

In `keep_pool()`, after `pool_expire()` and `pool_reap()` and before any staging:

    if workload_room() < workload_reserve:
        pick the evictable spare whose branch has the OLDEST `seen`, and among those with no
        `seen` at all, any; claim it with pool_take(); drop it; return.

Four properties, each of them load-bearing:

  * ONE PER PASS. The invariant is restored by one shed, so a second in the same pass is shedding
    for demand nobody has expressed. If the demand is real the next pass sees it again.
  * `pool_take()` FIRST, exactly as `pool_expire()` does. `out/owner` created O_EXCL is the only
    arbiter of whether a spare is free, and a shed that loses the race has lost to a dispatch
    already in progress -- which is a container the shed has no business touching.
  * `out/retiring` written, so ffstatus does not call it `claimed` and send an operator looking
    for a turn that does not exist. Same reason as the expiry path.
  * OLDEST FIRST, not newest, and A BRANCH WITH NO ACTIVITY AT ALL SORTS AHEAD OF ALL OF THEM.
    The list is "branches touched recently"; one that has dropped out of the window entirely is
    not a tiebreak case, it is the first thing to go. `activity.get(key) or ""` puts it there by
    construction, since the empty string sorts before any ISO timestamp.

And it returns before staging, so a pass never sheds and stages in the same breath.

ONE MORE ORDERING RULE, ON THE STAGING SIDE. `pool_stage` blocks the daemon's own loop for as long
as ffbox takes, up to the 180-second ceiling ffwatch kills it at, and the keeper already stages one
held spare per class per pass. So the evictable tier stages AT MOST ONE PER PASS across all
classes, and stages nothing at all on a pass that staged a held one. That bounds the loop's
blocking to what it is today, and it states the priority in the one place it can be enforced: a
promise is filled before a guess is made.

THE SHED NEVER TOUCHES A CLAIMED CONTAINER, and this is where the 2026-09-01 loss is the warning:
the `ffbox.pool` label survives the rename at dispatch, so ANY decision made from labels alone will
one day pick a container that is serving a live turn. The label narrows the candidates; `out/owner`
decides. `pool_drop()` refuses a claimed spool a second time, and the shed passes no `force`.


7. Thrash
----------

Stage, shed thirty seconds later, stage again: each cycle is a 22 GiB extraction and a chown over
89,664 files, and a box with CI arriving in bursts could sit in that loop all day. Two guards, both
cheap:

  * HYSTERESIS. Shed at `free < reserve`, stage at `free >= reserve + 2`. The gap is the dead band;
     a box oscillating by one place stages nothing and sheds nothing.
  * A PER-BRANCH COOLDOWN after a shed or a failed staging, `pool_stage_backoff_secs` (300).
    `_pool_stage_after` is keyed by class today and is RE-KEYED to `(class, branch)` for this:
    on one key, a branch whose staging failed would also stop that class's HELD pool being topped
    up, which is a guess blocking a promise. The held path's key is `(class, pool_branch(class))`.

Neither guard needs to be right to be safe. Getting them wrong costs extractions, never a turn.


8. Memory is the real ceiling
------------------------------

`max_concurrent_runs` counts containers; what actually runs out is RAM. A workspace is a tmpfs
Docker creates, 22 GiB for master, and `POOL_WORKSPACE_BYTES` (24 GiB) is what the keeper reserves
per place. `pool_has_room()` already asks the right question -- is there memory for this one plus
every cold launch the ceiling still allows -- and evictable staging goes through it unchanged.

Two evictable spares is ~48 GiB standing. On a box where `pool_has_room()` already declines, this
feature stages nothing at all and costs nothing but the query. That is the correct failure and it
needs no code: the existing guard runs first.

What DOES need saying is that the reserve and `pool_has_room()` guard different things and both
apply. The reserve is about places somebody else can take; `pool_has_room()` is about memory. A box
with places free and no memory stages nothing; a box with memory free and no places stages nothing.


9. Claiming and dispatch: nothing changes
------------------------------------------

`pool_claim_for()` already matches on class and exact branch, `pool_would_serve()` already asks the
same question without the side effect, and `schedule()` already exempts a dispatch from both
ceilings because handing a turn to a running container creates nothing. An evictable spare is
claimed by exactly that path, and from the moment `out/owner` exists it is a dispatched container
like any other: it is not evictable any more, because the shed skips anything with an owner, and
`pool_drop()` would refuse it even if the shed did not.

One consequence worth stating plainly: a turn dispatched into an evictable spare is a NORMAL turn.
Same task script, same job.json, same harvest, same destruction after one prompt. The tier is a
fact about how the container was scheduled, never about how the run behaves.


10. Clocks
-----------

An evictable spare gets its own TTL, `warm_branches.ttl_secs`, default 3600 -- matched to the
window it was chosen by. A branch nobody touched for an hour is not in the candidate list any more,
and a spare for it should not outlive its own reason by three hours.

Nothing new enforces it. `--idle-ttl` is already passed at stage time and lands in `out/staged` as
`ttl_secs`, `pool_expire()` already compares against it host-side, and the container already keeps
its own absolute failsafe at TTL plus fifteen minutes. This is a different NUMBER through the same
pipe.


11. What ffstatus and /status show
-----------------------------------

`pool_status()` prints one block per class today: "N staged, M wanted, on <branch>". It grows a
second line per class for the evictable tier, and the per-container rows gain the tier, so a
reader can tell a promise from a guess:

    pool ffdev: 1 staged, 1 wanted, on master
      a1b2c3d4  held       master              3h41m left
    pool ffdev warm branches: 2 of up to 2, window 1h
      e5f6a7b8  evictable  ffbox/comment-poll  52m left
      c9d0e1f2  evictable  discord/trust-tier  38m left

`ffstatus.sh` reads the container table from `docker ps` labels directly (ffstatus.sh:511-513) and
needs `ffbox.pool.tier` added to that format string, or its branch column will show two rows that
look identical and are not.

A shed should log one line naming the branch and the reason, because from the outside a spare
vanishing looks the same as a spare crashing:

    pool: shedding evictable ffbox/comment-poll (e5f6a7b8) -- 0 places free, reserve is 1

AND SO SHOULD EVERY DECLINE, which the first version of this did not do and which cost the first
hour it was live. Six conditions stop a warm-branch staging -- the dead band, a class ceiling, a
memory squeeze, a satisfied tier, a failed staging, and no candidate -- and all six were silent, so
"no spare appeared" had no diagnosis at all behind it. Whether the box was short of places, short
of memory, or simply had no branch worth warming looked identical, and those want completely
different responses.

    pool: not staging warm branches: 2 place(s) free, and one is only staged with 3
          (6 on the box, reserve 1, plus a place of hysteresis ...)
    pool: no warm-branch candidate for ffdev: 3 branch(es) wanted in the last 1.0 hours --
          1 on the pool branch already, 2 not in the mirror
    pool: not staging a warm branch for ffdev: it is at its own pool.max of 3

ONE LINE PER TRANSITION. The keeper runs every `poll_secs`, so an unconditional line is wallpaper
within minutes and wallpaper is how a real warning gets missed -- the same argument `_unreapable`
and `_held_logged` were written for, and `_pool_squeeze_logged` is the precedent on the held tier.
The latch is on the reason KEY and never on the message, because the message carries counts that
move between passes; "2 places free" becoming "3" is not news. The one exception is the
no-candidate key, which carries WHICH filters rejected everything -- "nothing has been touched" and
"everything there is was rejected by the mirror" are the same silence and different situations, and
one key for both would report the first and swallow the second.

The mirror one is worth calling out because it catches people: a branch missing from the mirror is
a CI fetch that has not happened, not anything about this pool.


12. Decisions still open
-------------------------

  a. WHETHER THE RESERVE SHOULD COVER HELD SPARES. Section 5. The latent problem is real and the
     fix changes what `idle: N` promises. Left alone here on purpose.
  b. WHETHER A SHED SHOULD PREFER THE OTHER CLASS'S SPARE when the demand is known to be one
     class's. It usually is not known -- CI and a cold `ffbox` are not a class -- and a rule that
     only sometimes has an input is a rule with two behaviours. Oldest-first, class-blind, until
     something measured says otherwise.
  c. WHETHER `warm_branches.count` SHOULD BE PER CLASS OR BOX-WIDE. Per class here, following
     every other pool number, at the cost of a box holding `count x classes` guesses -- two at the
     shipped default. If that turns out to be too many, the box-wide reserve is what actually
     stops it rather than the count, so the shape can stay either way.
  d. WHETHER A BRANCH WITH A CACHE ENTRY OF ITS OWN SHOULD BE PREFERRED. `ffbox`'s ladder takes
     `<branch>@<scope>.tar` when it exists and master's otherwise (ffbox:1140), so a branch CI has
     built for is meaningfully cheaper to stage. Not modelled; the ordering is recency only. Worth
     revisiting if staging cost turns out to bite.


13. Failure modes
------------------

  * THE MIRROR LOSES A BRANCH between the candidate query and `docker run`. The container dies in
    restore, the keeper reaps it, the cooldown holds it off for five minutes, and the next pass
    finds `mirror_carries()` false and drops it from the list. Costs one extraction.
  * THE DATABASE IS UNREADABLE on a pass. The candidate list is empty, no evictable spare is
    staged, existing ones live out their TTL. Degrades to today.
  * THE SHED CANNOT STOP A CONTAINER. `pool_drop()` logs and the invariant stays broken; the next
    pass tries the same spare again, and the container's own failsafe fires at TTL plus fifteen.
    A waiter waits longer, which is what it does today anyway.
  * A DRAIN ARRIVES. `draining()` already destroys every unclaimed spare of both tiers, by
    `out/owner` and not by label, and `keep_pool()` already returns early while draining. Nothing
    tier-specific to add.
  * `workload_reserve` SET HIGHER THAN `max_concurrent_runs`. Free places can never reach it, so
    the keeper sheds everything evictable and stages none. Clamp it to `max_concurrent_runs - 1`
    in `load_config` and say so, the way the other ceilings are clamped.


14. What this does not do
--------------------------

  * It does not make a cold run faster. A branch outside the window still pays forty seconds.
  * It does not warm a branch before the box has run a turn on it. The first turn of a
    conversation is cold on its own branch, always, because the branch does not exist yet.
  * It does not change what a run does, what it publishes, or what it is allowed to touch.
  * It does not give CI warm containers. CI fills its own workspaces on its own lane; all it gets
    here is the places back.
  * It does not add a second reason for a container to exist. Every staged container is still one
    prompt, one harvest, one destruction.
