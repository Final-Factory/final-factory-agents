ffcache — a host-local workspace cache for Final Factory CI
===========================================================

Replaces actions/cache in .github/workflows/main.yml with a cache that lives on the runner host
at /opt/ffcache: one tar archive per branch, holding the whole workspace, promoted by the
supervisor, restored read-only by the job.

Its second purpose is to retire ffbox's warm-library cron, which today keeps /opt/FinalFactory
warm by launching the Unity editor outside the hardened container. Once CI produces the archives,
golden is refreshed by extracting one, and no editor runs outside a container at all.

Written 2026-08-29. All measurements are from this host on that date. Companion to
design/ffgithubrunners_design.txt; "section N of the runner design" refers to that document.


1. What it replaces
-------------------

In .github/workflows/main.yml, three things that go together:

  :62-68    actions/cache/restore@v6, path Library, key
            Library-<projectPath>-<UNITY_VERSION>-<branch>-<run_id>, restore-keys falling back
            to the branch prefix and then the version prefix
  :157-161  actions/cache/save@v6, same key, `if: success()`
  :162-193  "Delete old caches, keeping only the most recent per branch" — 32 lines of
            `gh cache list` piped through inline python, then `gh cache delete` per id

Those are the only `gh` calls in the file (lines 168 and 192) and the only thing needing
`permissions: actions: write` at :9. Both go with them.

On the host it also replaces ffbox/04-warmLibrary.sh and its trigger. Section 12.

Unchanged: the LFS materialisation step (:33-56), the editor step, the check run, the log dump
and the two artifact uploads. The checkout at :30-32 changes in exactly one respect — section 7.


2. Why: what the current arrangement costs
------------------------------------------

Section 5 of the runner design measured twelve recent jobs:

    checkout 117s      LFS 21s      cache restore 108s      tests 456s

The first three are one activity — materialising a project that already exists on the machine
that wants it — and every job on this box downloads the same multi-gigabyte blob from Azure to
do it. Section 16 of that design deferred this explicitly: "if the 108s cache restore or the
117s checkout ever matters, revisit with data." This is the data.

Component sizes for this checkout, apparent bytes (what tar moves) against on-disk bytes (what
ZFS stores after lz4):

                    apparent   on disk   files
    .git               6.1 G     5.6 G             of which .git/lfs  4.9 G / 4.3 G
    worktree           7.2 G     5.6 G   26,679    3,190 of them LFS-tracked
    Library            8.6 G     4.4 G   59,405
    total             22   G    16   G

One archive covers all three costs. Measured for Library alone (9,280,010,240-byte archive,
3.8 G on disk after lz4): 26.94s to write from tmpfs, 17.42s to read back into tmpfs with the
archive in ARC. Extrapolated linearly to 22 G that is roughly 64s to save and 41s to restore.
The extrapolation is not measured and will not be exactly linear, but 41s against 246s is a
margin wide enough not to need a good estimate.


3. The format: one tar, not a directory tree
--------------------------------------------

The obvious design — copy the tree to and from /opt/ffcache — is wrong here, by a factor of
twenty, and the reason is the hardware. rpool is a mirror of sda4 and sdb4 behind a PERC H710,
and `lsblk -d -o NAME,ROTA` reports ROTA=1 for both: spinning disks.

    cp -a, ZFS -> tmpfs, Library as a tree      364.73 s
    tar -xf, ZFS -> tmpfs, same content          17.42 s

59,405 small file creates against a HDD mirror is what the first number is. A tar makes the same
data one sequential stream and confines the small-file problem to tmpfs, where it is free.

Consequences that follow from choosing tar:

  - The archive is NOT compressed by tar. The dataset is compressed instead, in kernel threads
    rather than in the job, and the ARC then caches the compressed form. lz4 gives 2.4:1 on the
    Library archive.
  - This host has 755 G of RAM with 644 G free, so the working set of the cache is ARC-resident
    in practice and 17.42s is the realistic read, not a best case. A genuinely cold read is
    bounded by mirror sequential throughput and has not been measured — open item (a).
  - A single file makes promotion an atomic rename(2), which is what removes every lock from the
    read path. Section 8.

Rejected on the same evidence: rsync delta saves. rsync is not in the image (verified: `command
-v rsync` in ffghrunner:latest is empty; flock, tar, cp and find are present) and a delta save
only helps a tree-shaped cache, which this section has just ruled out.

Also considered and rejected: baking the archive into the runner image. It removes the bind mount
entirely, which is attractive, but an image carries ONE snapshot refreshed weekly by
ffgithubrunners-image.timer, it cannot hold ten branches without putting 160 G of layers in a
64 G daemon store, and the thing that produces it still has to run the import somewhere. Section
12 gets the same "no job writes shared state" property by a different route.


4. What is in an archive
------------------------

The whole workspace: worktree, .git including .git/lfs, and Library. Not Library alone.

Including .git is what turns a 108s saving into a 246s one, and it is what lets the restore run
BEFORE actions/checkout, which then does an incremental fetch instead of a clone. Including
.git/lfs makes `git lfs pull` a local operation.

Excluded, always:

    Temp/  Logs/  UserSettings/  *-artifacts/
    Library/UnityLockfile  Library/EditorInstance.json
    .git/index.lock  .git/hooks/

`.git/hooks/` is excluded for the reason in section 11: git executes it, and an archive is
written by one job and extracted by the next.


5. The entry: naming and the three rules
----------------------------------------

    <sanitized branch>@<scope>.tar          develop@6000.3.19f1.tar

THE DELIMITER IS `@` AND THAT IS LOAD-BEARING. main.yml:59-61 sanitizes the branch with
`sed 's/[^a-zA-Z0-9._-]/-/g'`, so `@` cannot survive into the branch field: the host splits on
the FIRST `@` and the branch is unambiguous. `--` would not do — `feature/foo--bar` sanitizes to
`feature-foo--bar` and keeps its dashes. Verified: `user@host` -> `user-host`.

The scope is the Unity version. It is metadata, not identity. The one-per-branch rule below keys
on the branch field ALONE, so an editor upgrade replaces a branch's entry rather than
accumulating beside it, and the restore refuses an entry whose scope does not match its own
UNITY_VERSION — a PackageCache built by a different editor is worse than none.

Three rules, enforced by the host, in this order:

  1. ALWAYS A SINGLE TARBALL. One file per entry, no directories, no sidecar payloads.
  2. ONE ENTRY PER BRANCH. Promoting `<branch>@<scope>.tar` first deletes every other
     `<branch>@*.tar`, whatever its scope.
  3. THE LAST CACHE_KEEP ENTRIES SURVIVE, default 10, by the LRU clock in section 9.

Ten entries at 16 G on disk is 160 G. rpool has 2.1 T free; the dataset quota is 250G.


6. The mounts
-------------

Two, added to the existing `docker run` at slot.sh:145:

    -v $CACHE_DIR/entries:/ffcache:ro
    -v $CACHE_DIR/staging/slot-$SLOT:/ffghr/out

`/ffghr/out` mirrors what ffbox has had all along — 04-warmLibrary.sh passes
`-v "$OUT:/ffbox/out"` and import-project.sh writes its log there. It is deliberately a GENERAL
output mount rather than a cache-specific one: it gives ffgithubrunners the debugging channel it
currently lacks, and the cache archive is one well-known filename in it.

This is a departure from section 5 of the runner design ("Nothing is bind-mounted") and that
line needs amending. What survives of the property is in section 11.

Everything is skipped when CACHE_DIR is empty or the directory is absent, so a machine that has
not been provisioned runs jobs with no cache and no error.


7. Restore
----------

It lives in main.yml, inline. It does not need to be in the image.

A `run:` step executes before any checkout: the runner receives the workflow file from the
Actions service, not from the repository, and creates $GITHUB_WORKSPACE before the first step.
Proven on this machine — .github/workflows/ffghr-smoke.yml:26-32 is a `run:` step that reads
`df -h "$GITHUB_WORKSPACE"` at line 26, twenty lines before actions/checkout at :45, and the
README records that job succeeding on 2026-08-29.

So no image rebuild is ever needed to change caching behaviour, and the restore lands in the
same commit as the workflow that depends on it.

      - name: Restore the workspace from the host cache
        run: |
          ... inline, see below ...

      - uses: actions/checkout@v7
        with:
          lfs: true
          clean: false        # REQUIRED — see below

`clean: false` IS NOT AN OPTIMISATION. .gitignore:4 ignores `/[Ll]ibrary/`, and
actions/checkout's default `clean: true` runs `git clean -ffdx`, which deletes ignored files.
Restoring before a cleaning checkout hands the clean step a warm Library to delete. This
contradicts section 8 of the runner design ("the checkout keeps its default clean"), which needs
updating. It is safe for the same reason the ram disk is safe: the workspace is a fresh tmpfs per
container, so there is nothing to clean that this job did not itself put there.

What the step does:

  1. NO /ffcache -> print one line and exit 0. THE RESTORE MUST NEVER FAIL THE JOB. An
     unprovisioned machine, an unmounted cache, a missing entry, a truncated archive and a tar
     error are all the same outcome: a cold job, which is slow and correct. Every exit path in
     this step is 0.
  2. Choose an entry, in order:
       a. /ffcache/<branch>@<scope>.tar
       b. /ffcache/<default branch>@<scope>.tar
       c. the newest /ffcache/*@<scope>.tar
     Branch-only lookup was considered and rejected: a new branch would pay a full clone plus a
     42-minute cold import, and section 12's measurements show a stale-but-present Library costs
     one re-imported asset and eighty seconds of recompile after three days of drift. Today's
     restore-keys already fall back this way, so branch-only would be a regression against
     current behaviour rather than a tightening.
  3. Extract into $GITHUB_WORKSPACE with --no-same-owner --no-same-permissions.
  4. Sanitize the restored .git — section 11.
  5. Write the entry name used to /ffghr/out/used, so the host can bump the LRU clock for a job
     that restores and never saves.

Then actions/checkout does the git update. It is built for a pre-existing repository on a
persistent self-hosted workspace: it verifies the remote, fetches, and `git checkout --force`s
the wanted sha; on a remote mismatch it deletes the directory and re-clones, which is the right
failure mode for a corrupt entry. THIS IS THE ONE LOAD-BEARING ASSUMPTION NOT VERIFIED HERE.
Section 14.

The LFS materialisation step at main.yml:33-56 needs no change and earns its keep twice. With
.git/lfs in the archive `git lfs pull` finds its objects locally and costs nothing, and the
pointer scan after it is exactly the guard against a cached tree whose smudge went wrong — the
one new failure mode this design introduces. It was written for the old problem and covers the
new one for free.


8. Save, and promotion
----------------------

      - name: Stage the workspace for the host cache
        if: github.event_name == 'push'
        run: |
          ... inline ...

NOT `if: success()`. A branch whose tests fail should keep its warm workspace: the expensive
thing is the import, not the verdict. Today's `if: success()` at main.yml:158 means a branch
that fails re-imports from cold on every push until it goes green.

`if: github.event_name == 'push'` is the containment control. A pull_request run restores but
never writes, which is GitHub's own cache-write scoping, and it also stops PR refs (`123/merge`
sanitizes to `123-merge`) churning the ten slots that are meant to hold real branches.

The step writes /ffghr/out/ffcache.tar.part, renames it to ffcache.tar, and writes the intended
entry name to /ffghr/out/ffcache.name. A job killed mid-archive leaves only the .part, which the
host ignores and discards with the staging directory.

The host half runs in slot.sh's existing teardown trap (slot.sh:39), after the container is
removed so nothing is still writing, under flock on $CACHE_DIR/.prune.lock:

    name=$(cat $STAGE/ffcache.name)
    printf %s "$name" | grep -qE '^[A-Za-z0-9._-]+@[A-Za-z0-9._-]+\.tar$' || reject
    branch=${name%%@*}
    rm -f $CACHE_DIR/entries/"$branch"@*.tar        # rule 2
    mv    $STAGE/ffcache.tar $CACHE_DIR/entries/"$name"
    touch $CACHE_DIR/entries/"$name"                # LRU clock
    prune_to "$CACHE_KEEP"                          # rule 3
    rm -rf $STAGE

THE REGEX IS THE WHOLE PATH-TRAVERSAL DEFENCE. The job proposes a name; the host decides whether
that is a name. `..`, `/` and everything else outside the character class are rejected, and the
job never learns whether it was.

Promotion is rename(2) within one dataset: atomic and instantaneous, so a concurrent reader never
observes a partial entry and the read path needs no lock at all. The only lock in the system is
this one, and it exists because three slots are enabled
(~/.config/ffbox/githubrunners/config.json has "slots": 3).

Every step is best-effort and logged. A cache that fails to promote must never turn a passing job
into a failed slot.


9. The LRU clock
----------------

The entry's own mtime, touched on promotion and touched by the host when a job reports a restore
through /ffghr/out/used. That is what makes rule 3 "the last ten branches USED" rather than "the
last ten written": a branch that restores the cache and then fails, or that runs a job which does
not save, stays in the ten.

reap.sh runs the same prune under the same lock every fifteen minutes
(systemd/ffgithubrunners-reap.timer), so a machine whose slots stop exiting cleanly cannot grow
past CACHE_KEEP.


10. Host layout, permissions and the uid map
--------------------------------------------

    /opt/ffcache/                  own dataset, 0755, traversable by the daemon account
      entries/                     FinalFactoryTester:ffbox-container, 2770
        develop@6000.3.19f1.tar
      staging/                     FinalFactoryTester:ffbox-container, 2775 setgid
        slot-1/ slot-2/ slot-3/    created 0770 per job, wiped after
      .prune.lock

The dataset, following the pattern 01-hostSetup.sh:297-307 already uses for the daemon store:

    zfs create -o mountpoint=/opt/ffcache -o recordsize=1M -o compression=lz4 \
               -o atime=off -o sync=disabled -o quota=$CACHE_QUOTA <pool>/ff/ffcache

recordsize=1M because every object in it is a multi-gigabyte sequential file; sync=disabled
because it is a cache and losing it to a crash costs one cold job; the quota because a job writes
into staging and nothing else bounds it. A machine without ZFS gets a plain directory, exactly as
01-hostSetup.sh:285-289 already handles for the daemon root.

THE MODES LOOK ODD BECAUSE OF THE UID MAP, which was verified live rather than reasoned about. A
probe container against the real daemon with the real flag set (--cap-drop=ALL plus CHOWN,
FOWNER, DAC_OVERRIDE, --security-opt=no-new-privileges) and a bind-mounted host directory
reported `uid=0(root)` inside, and every file it created appeared on the host owned by uid 1020 —
ffbox-container, the rootless daemon's own account. flock on the bind mount worked. So:

  - The job writes into staging as host uid 1020. Nothing here can chown (the supervisor has no
    CAP_CHOWN), so the group bit is the entire mechanism: staging/ is setgid to ffbox-container,
    slot-N inherits that group, and uid 1020 writes through group rwx. FinalFactoryTester is a
    member of ffbox-container, so it can unlink what the job left without owning it.
  - entries/ is group-rwx for the same reason on the supervisor's side. The job cannot write there
    in spite of the mode, because the MOUNT is ro and the kernel enforces that before permissions
    are consulted.
  - The mount SOURCE must be traversable by uid 1020. The same probe against a path under a 0700
    directory failed with `error while creating mount source path ... permission denied`, which is
    what this looks like if the modes are ever loosened by mistake.

Configuration, in lib/config.sh's existing default-then-json-then-env form:

    CACHE_DIR    cache_dir    /opt/ffcache    empty disables the feature entirely
    CACHE_KEEP   cache_keep   10
    CACHE_QUOTA  cache_quota  250G

05-services.sh must substitute a @CACHEDIR@ placeholder into ffgithubrunners@.service and add it
to ReadWritePaths: the unit sets ProtectSystem=strict, so without that line the supervisor sees
/opt/ffcache read-only and every promotion fails.


11. Security properties
-----------------------

Section 12 of the runner design claims a job "cannot leave anything for the next job". After
this, that becomes: a job can leave exactly one tar archive, under a name the host validates, in
a directory the next job extracts into its workspace.

What is unchanged. The job gets one read-only mount and one per-slot drop box. It cannot delete
or alter an existing entry, cannot touch another slot's staging, cannot write outside CACHE_DIR,
cannot escape the entries directory by naming, and holds no capability it did not hold before.
Every host-side mutation is the supervisor's.

What is not new. Cache poisoning already exists: actions/cache is written by the job and restored
by the next job on the same branch, and Unity loads Library/ScriptAssemblies and Library/BurstCache
— which ARE the compiled repository code — on its first domain reload. Moving the blob from Azure
to /opt/ffcache does not create the class of attack. This is finding F8 in
docs/ci-runner-security-findings.md, which records it as a decision rather than a defect.

An archive is inert at rest and that is worth something — nothing executes a tar file, and the
host never traverses 59,405 attacker-named paths — but it is not what makes this acceptable. The
contents are executed by the consumer one job later, by design.

WHAT IS NEW, in two parts.

Scope. GitHub scopes cache writes: a feature branch can read master's entry but cannot write it.
Here a job can propose an entry under any branch name it likes. `if: github.event_name == 'push'`
narrows this to people who can push, who already hold the runner and the Unity credentials, but
it does not close it. Closing it would need the supervisor to independently verify which branch a
job is running, which it cannot.

A cached .git. This is finding F7 arriving through a different door: a previous job controls
`.git/config` and `.git/hooks/`, and git executes both — hooks on ordinary operations, and
`core.fsmonitor`, `core.pager` and `diff.external` as named commands that `git status` alone can
fire. In ffgithubrunners it does not escalate, because everything runs in the throwaway container
at the same trust level as the job that wrote the archive. It WOULD escalate anywhere the host
runs git in a restored tree, which is exactly what F7 describes ffbox doing at ffbox/ffbox:789.

So the restore sanitizes unconditionally, and it is three lines:

    rm -rf .git/hooks && mkdir -p .git/hooks
    for k in core.fsmonitor core.pager core.hooksPath diff.external \
             filter.lfs.process filter.lfs.smudge filter.lfs.clean; do
        git config --local --unset-all "$k" || :
    done
    git -c core.hooksPath=/dev/null -c core.fsmonitor=false ...    # every in-container git call

`.git/hooks/` is excluded from the archive as well (section 4), so the removal is belt to the
exclusion's braces. The lfs filter keys are on the list because they are the same mechanism, and
actions/checkout rewrites them anyway.


12. Retiring the warm-library cron
----------------------------------

This is the end state, and the reason the design is worth more than 200s a job.

WHAT HAPPENS TODAY. ffbox-update.timer fires every five minutes; when the fetch brings commits,
update_ffbox.sh:252 runs setup.sh --non-interactive, whose stage 4 (setup.sh:363-364) runs
04-warmLibrary.sh, which `docker run`s the ffbox image with `-v "$GOLDEN_MNT:/workspace"` — read
write, no hardening flags — and runs `unity-editor -quit -projectPath /workspace`.

That is arbitrary repository code executing, unattended, on FinalFactoryTester's daemon. Opening
the project IS running the project: the domain reload runs every [InitializeOnLoad] static
constructor in the tree (27 files match under Assets/; Assets/Editor/DevCommandWatcher.cs:25-29
is the plain unconditional case) and compilation runs the ILPostProcessors that
com.unity.entities 1.4.7 and com.unity.burst 1.8.29 install (Packages/manifest.json:20-23).

Measured: FinalFactoryTester's docker context is `rootless` -> unix:///run/user/1015/docker.sock,
and ffbox-update.service pins that path in its environment. A probe container on that daemon with
/opt/FinalFactory mounted reports `id -u` = 0 and `stat /workspace` = uid 0, gid 0 — so
ffbox/entrypoint.sh takes its `if [ "$uid" -eq 0 ]` shortcut, the UID dance its comments describe
never happens on this box, and the import runs as namespace root, which is host uid 1015: the
account holding ~/.git-credentials, ~/.claude/.credentials.json, secrets.env, and a place in the
sudo group. That is finding F1's outstanding half, reached by a path the finding does not name.

WHAT REPLACES IT. Golden stops being warmed by an editor and starts being warmed by an extract:

    tar -xf $CACHE_DIR/entries/<default branch>@<scope>.tar -C /opt/FinalFactory Library

Data movement, not code execution. No container, no licence, no editor, nothing to escape from.
04-warmLibrary.sh's import is deleted, stage 4 of setup.sh becomes the extract, and the
five-minute path stops launching Unity entirely. ffbox runs still clone a golden that carries a
warm Library, exactly as they do now.

TWO RULES KEEP IT SAFE.

  - GOLDEN CONSUMES ONLY THE DEFAULT BRANCH'S ENTRY. A feature-branch entry would be a real
    escalation: a CI job, contained at ffbox-container trust, would plant a Library that ffbox's
    editor later loads as uid 1015. The default branch's entry is not an escalation, because
    ffbox already executes the default branch's code by construction — that is what golden is.
  - GOLDEN EXTRACTS Library/ ONLY, never .git or the worktree. update-golden.sh owns those, and
    it is precisely the host-side git of section 11 that must never meet a job-written .git.

ORDERING. The cron cannot be retired until CI has produced a default-branch entry and the extract
is proven, so: land sections 1-11, let develop build an entry, prove the extract into golden,
then delete the import. `ffgithubrunners cache seed` should tar golden's existing warm Library
into the default-branch entry so the first CI job is not cold either.

WHILE HERE, a factual error to fix. update_ffbox.sh justifies its unconditional setup.sh call by
saying "the Unity warm skips outright when golden already has a Library/". It does not:
04-warmLibrary.sh logs "Library/ already present ... re-importing to pick up the changes just
pulled" and falls through with no exit. Every update opens the editor. ~/ffbox-runs/warm-* holds
eleven such runs between 2026-08-16 and 2026-08-28.


13. Cutover
-----------

Harness-side first, and it is inert until main.yml changes, because a workflow that does not
write /ffghr/out leaves nothing to promote and the current actions/cache steps keep working.

  1. 01-hostSetup.sh: the dataset, entries/, staging/, the modes in section 10, and --check
     output for all of it.
  2. lib/config.sh: CACHE_DIR, CACHE_KEEP, CACHE_QUOTA; config.json.example alongside.
  3. slot.sh: staging setup before the run at :145, the two -v arguments, and
     promote/bump/prune/wipe in the teardown trap at :39.
  4. 05-services.sh and systemd/ffgithubrunners@.service: @CACHEDIR@ in ReadWritePaths.
  5. reap.sh: the same prune under the same lock.
  6. `ffgithubrunners status`: cache directory, entry count, total size, oldest entry age.
     `ffgithubrunners cache list|seed|clear`.
  7. ffghr-smoke: prove section 14's first item before touching main.yml.
  8. main.yml: delete :62-68, :157-193 and `actions: write` at :9; add `clean: false` to the
     checkout at :30-32; add the restore step before it and the save step after the editor.
  9. Let develop produce an entry. Then section 12: prove the golden extract, then delete the
     import from 04-warmLibrary.sh and fix the update_ffbox.sh comment.

Steps 1-6 and 8 are separable in a way the runs-on cutover was not, because every path exits 0
when the mounts are absent.


14. Acceptance
--------------

  - actions/checkout@v7 reuses a restored .git rather than deleting it, with clean: false. This
    is the assumption everything else rests on and it is unverified. Prove it on ffghr-smoke,
    against a seeded entry, before step 8.
  - A cold job (empty cache) succeeds and produces an entry.
  - A warm job restores, and checkout reports a fetch rather than a clone.
  - Restore with /ffcache absent, with no matching entry, and with a truncated archive: all three
    produce a passing job.
  - Three concurrent jobs on three branches promote three entries, and a fourth branch evicts by
    LRU at CACHE_KEEP=3.
  - A job proposing `../../etc/x@y.tar` is rejected by the host and the job still passes.
  - Restore and save wall times against section 2's estimates, and the whole-job time against the
    twelve-job baseline in section 5 of the runner design.


15. Open items
--------------

  (a) A genuinely cold restore has not been measured. Every read in section 2 came out of a 755 G
      ARC. The number for a machine with less RAM, or right after a reboot, is unknown.
  (b) The 22 G extrapolation in section 2 is linear from a 9.28 G measurement. Measure the real
      archive once one exists.
  (c) Drift ACROSS branches is unmeasured. Section 12's evidence is golden drifting over TIME on
      one branch: after three days, one asset re-imported, 29s of recompile, 31s of domain
      reload. A feature branch may differ from the default branch by more than three days of
      develop does, and that is the number that decides how much the fallback in section 7 buys.
  (d) Whether the archive should exclude Library/PackageCache (1.6 G) and let UPM re-resolve. It
      would shrink entries meaningfully and re-resolving is an online operation the allowlist
      already covers, but it trades disk for a network dependency in every job.


16. Not in scope
----------------

  - deploy.yml, which uses actions/cache with key `Library-<targetPlatform>` at :45-49 and runs
    on `self-hosted`, so it keeps landing on the legacy runners and is unaffected.
  - The Windows determinism runner.
  - Moving ffbox onto ffbox-container's daemon. Section 12 removes the warm path's execution but
    ffbox's own runs still execute repository code on FinalFactoryTester's daemon; section 17 of
    the runner design is the plan for that and this design neither helps nor hinders it.
  - Narrowing who may write an entry beyond `push` events. Section 11 states the residue.
